## About this book

This book provides supplementary teaching materials for CBE4427/9133. Regular lectures develop the concepts, methods, and engineering reasoning; this book supports coding, data processing, and practice through chemical engineering examples. Your instructor will identify the relevant sections and activities.

Some materials are adapted from John Kitchin's *Data Science and Machine Learning in Chemical Engineering*. See [Attribution and license](attribution.md).

## Assessment

Use the assessment scheme for the course in which you are registered. The official course outlines and instructor announcements on OWL Brightspace govern course requirements.

| Component | Undergraduate (4427) | Graduate (9133) |
|---|---|---|
| Assessed assignments | 50% of the final course grade | 40% of the final course grade |
| Course project, total | 50% of the final course grade | 60% of the final course grade |
| Project report | Included in the 50% project component | 30% of the final course grade |
| Project presentation | Included in the 50% project component | 30% of the final course grade |

The report and presentation rows are the components of the project total, not additional weights. The undergraduate outline specifies the combined project weight but does not specify the report/presentation split; follow the instructor's project brief for that breakdown.

### Five assessed assignments

Both course outlines specify **five assignments**. The **lowest assignment grade, including a missed assignment, is dropped** when calculating the overall assignment grade. The assignment category then contributes the percentage shown above. Follow OWL Brightspace for the assessed assignment questions, due dates, and submission instructions.

### Tutorials and class exercises

The [13 tutorial notebooks](assignments/index.md) support tutorial sessions and practice. They are separate from the five assessed assignments. Your instructor will select the activities to complete. The book does not assign a separate course-grade weight to tutorials, class exercises, quizzes, or optional badge activities. Completing a notebook or a website quiz does not automatically submit work for assessment.

### Course project

The course project has two assessed components: an in-depth **written report** and a **research-seminar-style presentation**. Students identify a complex, real-world chemical engineering problem and develop an AI-based solution, supported by research, data, model implementation, and a comprehensive analysis of the results. The presentation should address the problem background, methodology, key findings, and innovative aspects of the solution.

The [project resources](assignments/project.ipynb) provide planning templates. Proposal and check-in templates are available for feedback when requested by the instructor; this book does not allocate separate marks to them. Suggested structures, page lengths, slide counts, and checklists are planning aids. Detailed marking criteria, presentation length, required files, and deadlines are specified in the instructor's project brief on OWL Brightspace.

## Course policies

Attendance at lectures and tutorials is mandatory under both outlines. Consult the official outline for late or missed work, academic consideration and accommodation, written-English requirements, and other course policies.

**Generative AI:** tools such as ChatGPT may be used for an assessed assignment or project only when its instructions explicitly allow them. The presence of AI-related examples or tools in this book does not grant permission to use them in assessed work.

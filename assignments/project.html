Course Project: AI in Chemical Engineering#

CBE4427/9133

Course

Project weight

Undergraduate (4427)

50% of the final course grade; report and presentation combined

Graduate (9133)

60% of the final course grade: report 30% and presentation 30%

The undergraduate outline does not specify the report/presentation split. Follow the instructor’s project brief for detailed component weights and marking criteria. See Course information for the complete assessment summary.

Overview#

Identify a complex, real-world problem in chemical engineering and develop an AI-based solution. Your work should include research, suitable data, model implementation, and a comprehensive analysis of the results. Explain the relevant engineering context, physical assumptions, and limitations, and communicate the innovative aspects of your solution.

Project Options#

Option A: Bring Your Own Data#

Use data from your research, internship, or a published paper. This is the preferred option as it connects to your own work.

Option B: Public Dataset#

Use a publicly available dataset relevant to chemical engineering:

  • UCI Machine Learning Repository

  • Kaggle Datasets

  • Materials Project

  • NIST Chemistry WebBook

Example Topics#

  • Predicting material properties from composition

  • Process optimization using surrogate models

  • Fault detection in manufacturing processes

  • Reaction yield prediction

  • Catalyst performance modeling

  • Polymer property prediction

Assessed project components#

Written report#

  • Define and motivate the chemical engineering problem.

  • Describe the research background, data, and methods.

  • Present model implementation, results, and a comprehensive written analysis.

  • Explain engineering implications, innovative aspects, and limitations.

Project presentation#

  • Present the problem background, methodology, key findings, and innovative aspects in a research-seminar format.

  • Follow the instructor’s instructions for presentation duration, questions, and required materials.

Planning and feedback templates#

The proposal and progress check-in notebooks are available to help organize your work and obtain feedback when requested. They are not assigned separate course-grade weights in this book. Follow the project brief on OWL Brightspace for deadlines, required submissions, and marking criteria.

Suggested report structure#

Adapt this planning outline to the instructor’s report requirements. Page lengths are suggestions, not assessment rules:

1. Introduction (1-2 pages)#

  • Problem motivation

  • Background and prior work

  • Objectives

2. Data (1-2 pages)#

  • Data source and collection

  • Feature descriptions

  • Exploratory data analysis

  • Preprocessing steps

3. Methods (2-3 pages)#

  • Model selection rationale

  • Hyperparameter tuning approach

  • Validation strategy

4. Results (2-3 pages)#

  • Model performance metrics

  • Comparison of methods

  • Feature importance / interpretability

  • Uncertainty quantification (if applicable)

5. Discussion (1-2 pages)#

  • Key findings

  • Limitations

  • Future work

6. Conclusions (0.5 page)#

  • Summary of contributions

Project planning checklist#

These prompts support preparation and self-review. They are not a separately weighted grading rubric; the instructor’s project brief specifies the marking criteria.

Area

Questions to check

Problem and background

Is the real-world chemical engineering problem clearly motivated and grounded in relevant research?

Data and implementation

Are the data suitable and the computational work reproducible?

Methods and validation

Are the AI methods justified and evaluated appropriately?

Results and analysis

Are results explained comprehensively, including uncertainty and limitations where relevant?

Engineering interpretation

Are physical assumptions, practical implications, and innovative aspects discussed?

Communication

Are the report and seminar presentation clear, well organized, and properly referenced?

Tips for Success#

  1. Start early - Data collection and cleaning take time

  2. Iterate - Don’t expect perfect results on the first try

  3. Document everything - Keep notes on what you tried

  4. Validate results - Check if findings make physical sense

  5. Ask questions - Office hours are there to help

  6. Focus on insight - A simple model with good interpretation beats a complex black box

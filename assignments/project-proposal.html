Project Proposal#

Due Date: See course schedule

Purpose#

The proposal ensures you have a clear direction for your project before investing significant time. It helps identify potential issues early and allows for feedback on your approach.

What You Need to Do#

1. Problem Description (1 paragraph)#

Clearly describe the problem you want to solve:

  • What is the chemical engineering (or your major if it is not chemical engineering) context?

  • Why is this problem important?

  • What question are you trying to answer?

2. Data Source and Description#

Describe your data:

  • Where does the data come from? (your research, public dataset, etc.)

  • How many samples do you have (or expect to have)?

  • What are the features (inputs)?

  • What is the target variable (output)?

  • Are there any known data quality issues?

3. Proposed Methods#

Outline your analysis plan:

  • What preprocessing steps do you anticipate?

  • Which ML methods do you plan to try? Why are they appropriate?

  • How will you validate your models?

4. Expected Outcomes#

State what you hope to achieve:

  • What would a successful outcome look like?

  • How will you measure success?

  • What insights do you hope to gain?

What Success Looks Like#

A successful proposal will:

Criterion

Expectation

Clarity

Problem is clearly stated and understandable

Relevance

Problem connects to chemical engineering

Feasibility

Data is accessible and sufficient for ML

Scope

Project is appropriately sized (not too big, not too small)

Methods

Proposed approaches are reasonable for the problem

Metrics

Clear criteria for evaluating success

Red Flags (things to avoid)#

  • Vague problem statements (“I want to use ML on chemical data”)

  • No data source identified

  • Data with fewer than ~50 samples (unless justified)

  • Methods that don’t match the problem (e.g., classification for a regression task)

  • No clear success criteria
